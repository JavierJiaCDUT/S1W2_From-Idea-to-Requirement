Problem or Solution?

A / PROPOSAL

“Build a Booking App.”

It names a product form. It does not tell us who struggles or what goes wrong.

B / PROBLEM CANDIDATE

“I Cannot Tell If the Room Is Booked.”

A student arranging group work reports uncertainty after requesting a room.

Which statement describes a difficulty that still needs investigation?

A problem statement can be specific without claiming it is already proven.

A problem candidate identifies a user and a difficulty without locking the team into a technology. A report from one user is a useful clue, not proof that every student has the same need.

What Is Still an Assumption?

Problem candidate: Students arranging group work cannot tell whether a requested room is confirmed, so they contact staff before sharing a meeting location.
USER + CONTEXT

Group Organisers

Arranging a room for a group meeting.

DIFFICULTY

Unclear Status

Cannot distinguish a request from a confirmed booking.

CONSEQUENCE

Extra Checking

Contact staff before telling the group where to meet.

Select the claim above. What evidence would you need before accepting it?

A problem statement combines a user, context, difficulty and consequence. Each part can still require evidence. Do not turn a desired benefit into a guaranteed outcome.

Whose Needs Matter?

STUDENT / GROUP ORGANISER

Can We Rely on This Room?

Needs to know whether the room is secured before inviting the group.

FACILITIES / ROOM MANAGER

Can We Trust the Schedule?

Stakeholders include people who use the service and people who operate or are affected by it. A request for speed must be considered alongside availability and booking rules. This case does not claim all stakeholder needs have been discovered.

02 / Read the source carefullyCHC5056 / 2026–2027

What Does This Evidence Tell Us?

E1 / INTERVIEW

“After I send a request, I email reception to ask whether we have the room.”

One fictional student’s account; not a measure of prevalence.
E2 / OBSERVATION

In one simulated booking attempt, the student received “request sent” and then contacted reception.

Observed sequence; it does not establish the person’s reason.
E3 / INTERVIEW

A fictional manager reports resolving requests for the same room and time.

A reported workflow issue; check records and rules.

All E1–E3 materials are simulated. In a real project, keep the source, context and limits of each finding. Observation can show an action; an interview can explore its meaning. Neither automatically establishes how common it is.

03 / Requirements elicitationCHC5056 / 2026–2027

Choose the Question First

Elicitation means investigating needs, constraints and expectations.

LEADING QUESTION

“Would a Booking App Help You?”

Introduces our solution and invites agreement.

NEUTRAL QUESTION

“Tell Me About Your Last Room Request.”

Follow up: “What happened next?” and “How did you know it was confirmed?”

Question→Stakeholder→Method→Limitations
Choose a PurposeAre we investigating a person’s reasoning, their actual steps, or how widely an issue is reported?

Choose a method after deciding what you need to learn. Neutral questions invite accounts of real situations without pushing participants towards your preferred feature. Do not collect sensitive or personal information for this classroom exercise.

Choose a Method

MethodUseful forWatch for
InterviewExploring reasons, context and experiences.Recall errors, leading prompts, limited reach.
ObservationSeeing actual steps and workarounds.Behaviour may change; motives remain unclear.

Scenario A: Why does a student email reception after requesting a room?

There is no context-free ranking of research methods. Judge the choice by the question, access to stakeholders and the limitations you can manage. A survey describes its respondents; wider claims depend on sampling.

03 / Explain the trade-offCHC5056 / 2026–2027

What Could This Method Miss?

Scenario B: You have a list of recent room requesters. How widespread is uncertainty about confirmation among them?
DOCUMENT ANALYSIS

Check the Rules

Read booking policies and existing records.

COMPETITOR ANALYSIS

Explore Alternatives

Inspect other services; do not treat their features as proof of our users’ needs.

FOCUS GROUP

Hear Different Views

Explore reactions together; dominant voices may shape the discussion.

Combine methods where useful. Documents can clarify constraints; competitor analysis suggests possibilities; focus groups expose differing perspectives but can be shaped by group dynamics. None substitutes for evidence about your intended users.

04 / Make the reasoning visibleCHC5056 / 2026–2027

From Findings to Requirements

E1 + E2

Finding: uncertainty is followed by extra checking in this simulated case.

N1 / USER NEED

Know whether the room is secured before telling the group where to meet.

R1 / CANDIDATE

Show whether a room request is confirmed or unsuccessful, with the room and time.

H1 / Still a hypothesis: clearer status will reduce follow-up checking. Investigate this effect.

Business goal: reduce avoidable enquiries. User need: know the booking outcome.
Related candidate: R0, show available time slots. Neither feature is proven by its wording.

R1 is a design response to N1, not a direct quotation from a user. The evidence supports investigating uncertainty; it does not prove that the proposed response will produce the intended benefit. R0 and R1 remain candidates.

04 / Classify and clarifyCHC5056 / 2026–2027

Behaviour or Quality?

R1 / EXPECTED BEHAVIOUR

Show the Booking Outcome

Display whether the request is confirmed or unsuccessful, with room and time.

Q1 / TEACHING TARGET

Show It Within Two Seconds

In a defined test environment, at least 95% of 1,000 requests render the outcome within two seconds at 50 concurrent users.

“The system must be fast” lacks a threshold and test conditions.
Q1 figures are invented for teaching. Full test setup must be agreed before execution.

Functional requirements describe required behaviour; non-functional requirements constrain qualities such as performance. Testability needs measurable criteria and a reproducible context. Numeric precision alone does not make a target justified.

A Story Expresses User Value

US1 · BASED ON N1 / R1 · REVEAL EACH PART
As a student organising a group meeting,
I want to see the outcome of my room request,
so that I can tell my group whether we have a room.

“Build the database” is an implementation task. It does not explain the user’s goal or value.

A story connects a role, a goal and an intended value. The template is a useful prompt rather than a complete specification. US1 expresses a candidate response to N1; stakeholders still need to discuss its details and value.

06 / Acceptance criteria for US1CHC5056 / 2026–2027

How Will We Know It Works?

Simulation rule B1: one room and time slot can have at most one confirmed booking.

AC1 / SUCCESS

The Room Is Available

Given an eligible request and a slot still available at confirmation,

when the request is processed,

then one booking is recorded and the student sees “confirmed”, room and time.

AC2 / CONFLICT

The Room Was Taken

Given the requested slot is already confirmed for another booking,

when this request is processed,

then no extra booking is recorded and the student sees “unsuccessful: slot taken”, room and time.

B1 is a fictional rule, not a university policy. Given / When / Then is one useful format.

AC1 and AC2 describe observable outcomes for the same story under different conditions. A team must also clarify eligibility, processing and other cases before implementation. These examples introduce acceptance criteria, not a complete test specification.

06 / Recognise observable expectationsCHC5056 / 2026–2027

Which Criteria Can We Test?

A

“Booking Is Easy.”

“The system works well and users are happy.”

B

“A Conflict Is Reported.”

When a requested slot is already taken, no extra booking is recorded and the outcome identifies the conflict, room and time.

Which criterion lets two people check the same expected outcome?

An acceptance criterion should support a repeatable decision about expected behaviour. The software can meet a criterion while the wider user problem persists. User value therefore still needs evidence.
